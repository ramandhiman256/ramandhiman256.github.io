I am a fifth-year Ph.D. candidate in the McKetta Department of Chemical Engineering at The University of Texas at Austin, co-advised by Dr. Manish Kumar and Dr. Benny Freeman. My dissertation, *Biomimetic Membranes for Neuromorphic Computing*, sits at the intersection of membrane science, biophysics, and unconventional computing.

The brain computes with ion channels and lipid membranes rather than transistors. I build materials that work the same way. Using droplet interface bilayer electrophysiology, I characterize ion transport, selectivity, and voltage dependence in artificial lipid membranes, and relate pore and peptide structure to conductance behavior. Two results anchor this work: blocker molecules can induce nonlinear conductance and emergent memristance in otherwise ohmic open pores, extending memristive dynamics beyond voltage-gated channels, and jammed interconnected bilayer emulsions reproduce voltage-dependent memory behavior in a 3D-printable tissue mimic. I am now developing more stable brain-mimicking bilayer networks and screening protein peptide combinations for physical neuromorphic and reservoir computing architectures.

I came to this from separations. My early work developed biomimetic membranes using engineered outer membrane proteins, and I continue to contribute membrane characterization and electrophysiology to collaborative projects on lanthanide-selective artificial channels, dehydrated biomimetic membranes, and protective carbon fabrics. Neuroscience coursework redirected my interest to the role of membrane proteins in learning and memory and, from there, to building them into devices.

I completed my undergraduate studies in Chemical Engineering at IIT Gandhinagar, where I received the Institute Silver Medal. My work at UT Austin has been supported by six endowed graduate fellowships, and I was named the McKetta Department's Outstanding Teaching Assistant for 2025–2026. I have mentored eight graduate, undergraduate, and intern researchers.
