  • Aug 2022 - Present

    Austin, TX

    Graduate Research Assistant
    The University of Texas at Austin
    McKetta Department of Chemical Engineering. Advisors: Manish Kumar and Benny D. Freeman.
    • Characterize ion transport, selectivity, and voltage dependence in artificial lipid membranes using droplet interface bilayer electrophysiology, relating pore and peptide structure to conductance behavior.
    • Demonstrated the emergence of memristance in jammed interconnected bilayer emulsions using alamethicin, establishing that a 3D-printable tissue mimic reproduces voltage-dependent memory behavior (Nature Materials, 2026).
    • Showed that blocker molecules induce nonlinear conductance and emergent memristance in open pores that otherwise exhibit ohmic current–voltage behavior, extending memristive dynamics beyond voltage-gated channels (first-author, Faraday Discussions, 2026).
    • Developing more stable brain-mimicking bilayer networks and evaluating pore and peptide combinations for neuromorphic computing applications.
    • Contributed membrane characterization and electrophysiology to collaborative projects on lanthanide-selective artificial channels, dehydrated biomimetic membranes, and protective carbon fabrics.
    • Mentored eight graduate, undergraduate, and intern researchers; four are co-authors on peer-reviewed publications.
